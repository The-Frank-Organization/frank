## Battle report — Governance-Decay class instance #2 (2026-07-22, from the m-9/m-10 §D join settlement)

**Finding (m-9-discovered, m-10-confirmed):** a `parked_unknown` safety disclosure lands in **assembled content**, not the Tier-0 pinned set, so the ratified worker compaction (Tier-1 eviction / Tier-2 summarization) can **evict** it — the model could then act turns later un-informed of a parked unknown effect. Under the ratified *run-wide* D-4 mechanism the next turn's frame silently re-attached the disclosure (worker-independent by construction, r40 `:72`); the defect was reachable only under a rev6 *turn-scoped* re-scoping (since corrected — see the §D-settlement amendment carrying the run-wide restore + `MAX_PARKED_ROWS_PER_RUN` cap).

**General lesson (adopt into positioning + design review):** **when a compactor can evict a governance fact, prefer RE-DELIVERY from the authoritative producer over RE-DERIVATION by the affected consumer.** m-9's candidate consumer-side repair (re-surface from the worker's durable log) is weakest exactly on the `content_lost`/`DEGRADED` path — where the consumer's own durable state is what failed; m-10's producer-side re-delivery (attach unconditionally from durable state every turn) holds precisely there. ## Battle report — relay timestamp drift, and the R-4.3 append-contention dependency transferred onto frank (2026-07-25, from the harness + pdc index cutovers)

**The disease.** Relay filename stamps and index `time` cells were never validated — `relay-lint` parsed the stamp *only* for sort order. Measured at cutover: **harness/master** carried 110 rows stamped in the future (newest ~3.4 days ahead), 297 non-monotonic adjacent pairs, 2 impossible times (`20260712-016200`, minute 62 — one a real relay *file*), 3 rows disagreeing with the filename they point at, and a synthetic exactly-hourly cadence. **pdc** independently reported 627/1251 relay stamps more than ±2 min from their true git landing time and 83 inversions already in-file. Two projects, same failure, found independently.

**Root cause (pdc's diagnosis, adopted — it is the sharper framing).** The `time` column had become **a sequence key wearing a timestamp**: with concurrent sessions appending to one shared append-only file, seats **re-stamped upward to win append races**. The stamp stopped being an observation of a clock and became a tie-breaker. Once that happens the trail is unorderable in the way that matters — a relay can appear to precede its own parent — and the damage is unrepairable after the fact, because those wrong stamps are the *only* record of authoring order that exists and every citation names a filename stamp.

**Mechanized (relay-lint v2.8.8.2, `iwnlcern/agentic-dev-team-skills@2b766cd`).** Impossible stamps rejected in every mode; wall-clock drift (±15 min default) rejected on the authoring path only — a `--relay-root` sweep never checks drift, since history is legitimately old; and `--index` checks real times, pairwise monotonicity, floor, and filename agreement. History is grandfathered by a **positional** `<!-- relay-lint: monotonic-from <stamp> -->` marker, positional because a value-based cutoff is defeated by pre-existing future-stamped rows (they would force every later row to keep matching the fabrication instead of the real clock). Enforcement is forward-only by the marker's **line position**, *not* by only checking the newest row — every post-marker row is checked pairwise. **Reusable pattern, adopted from pdc:** an honest labeled `ADMIN`/`index-timestamp-cutover` row immediately above the marker, stamp-aligned, so the discontinuity is a record in the append-only trail rather than a silent edit. Both projects' indexes now verify `OK` with zero history repaired.

**The dependency now on us — pdc's R-4.3.** pdc has **closed its transport redesign out of scope** by operator ruling (per-seat index shards vs a serialized canonical appender), on the basis that **frank's conductor + harness runtime owns that cause**, with a single re-open condition: it returns if the upstream runtime does not in fact remove the contention. Flagged to us deliberately, so the dependency is visible from this side.

**Assessment (verified against `frank/` source, not assumed) — frank does remove the cause, for traffic that flows through it.** Three independent mechanisms already do exactly what R-4.3's serialized-appender option would have built: the conductor is a **single-threaded serialized commit loop** (m-7's domain) with crash-atomic multi-file commit; order is assigned server-side by a **monotonic intake sequence** (`intake-%06d`), never by a client-supplied value; and the FieldSpec registry declares `timestamp` as `owner: system` / `fill_constraints: system_only` / `lineage_role: none`, while the record `Envelope` carries **no author-supplied time field at all**. So a seat *cannot* stamp, there is no race to win by re-stamping, and time is never a gate input. pdc's ruling is well-founded.

**The scope boundary that must not be lost (stated back to pdc, and a live constraint on the re-open condition).** frank removes this cause **only for relays that actually flow through `submit`/`project`/`read`**. A hand-authored markdown `INDEX.md` maintained by concurrent sessions sits **outside** frank's boundary — so for any team still hand-relaying, the contention persists and the v2.8.8.2 checks are the only guard. This is not hypothetical for pdc: their relays are files today.

**Hardening carry (Step-4-shaped, no MVP claim).** frank has **no human-readable ordered relay index** — the natural replacement for a hand-maintained `INDEX.md` is a **projection** over the store ordered by intake sequence and system-stamped, which would make this drift class *structurally impossible* rather than merely linted. Two items: (a) provide that index-replacement projection as a first-class surface so a team migrating onto frank stops maintaining the file by hand; (b) treat "the governance artifact a team edits concurrently by hand" as a general hazard class — wherever the ecosystem still asks humans/agents to hand-append to a shared ordered file, the ordering key must be produced by the courier, never by the author. The team's friction IS the product roadmap: this is the second concrete case (after the false-routing check) where a drafting hazard should become a courier guarantee.

## Battle report — the MCP `form_digest` is unreachable from a foreign harness (2026-07-25, lane-4 preflight, FIRST live dogfood of the nested team)

**What happened.** All three preflight seats blocked on their first governed submit. `l4.orchestrator-planner` (Claude Code) was rejected once, recovered, and landed hop 1. `l4.orchestrator-reviewer` (Codex) was rejected and **could not recover at all** — it re-read its tool schema, got the identical digest, resubmitted, and received the identical rejection. It stopped rather than inventing a value, which is the correct behaviour and is why this was diagnosable.

**Root cause (read from source, then confirmed by a live accepted submit).** Two facts combine into a trap:
1. `cmd/frank-mcp/mcp.go` advertises the submit schema rendered at a **hardcoded `(PHASE=SITREP, CEREMONY_TIER=medium)`** (`toolsListResult` → `refreshSubmitSchema(client, "SITREP", "medium")`).
2. The conductor recomputes the expected digest from the **record's own** phase/tier — `Registry.Render(env, seat, cand.Headers["PHASE"], cand.Headers["CEREMONY_TIER"], grants)` in `classifyBootAdmission` / `Validate`.

So the advertised const is valid **only** for a submit that happens to declare `SITREP`/`medium`. Any other tier mismatches *by construction*, on a first attempt, with no user error. The digest is a **form-freshness proof whose validity silently depends on caller-chosen fields**.

**Why the recovery path is harness-dependent — the actual defect.** On a `re-render` rejection the shim re-renders for the *declared* phase/tier and updates its cached schema, expecting the client to re-read `tools/list`. **Claude Code honours the mid-session tool-schema change; Codex does not.** The planner recovered on attempt 2; the reviewer was stuck in a permanent loop, holding a stale const with **no path forward from inside the seat**. This directly contradicts a stated Step-3 MVP goal — *"MCP retained for foreign harnesses"* — because the one recovery mechanism assumes a client capability the MVP never required. A protocol whose only escape hatch is an optional client feature does not work for foreign harnesses.

**Second finding — the rejection class is wrong, and misleadingly so** (raised independently by the planner). A wrong-value digest and a genuinely stale form are reported as the same class `re-render` with the hint *"form refreshed — re-read the submit tool schema and re-submit."* The form had **not** refreshed. An operator who trusts the hint re-reads an unchanged schema and resubmits the same wrong value forever — the exact loop the reviewer hit. Split into `digest-mismatch` vs `form-refreshed`.

**On echoing the expected digest (the seats' proposed fix) — accept the goal, not the mechanism.** Returning the expected digest on rejection would unblock any client, but it hands over the very value the field exists to prove was *read from the rendered form*, reducing the check to copy-the-number. Prefer, in order: (a) make the advertised schema not depend on caller-chosen fields — render the digest over the form's *invariant* shape, or bind it per (seat, phase, tier) and advertise the set; or (b) have the shim render per-call from the declared headers before forwarding, so the client never holds a stale const; or (c) if a hint must carry recovery data, return the *declared* phase/tier the digest was computed against, which is diagnostic without being the answer.

**Third finding — no roster introspection.** `project` with `view: roster` returns `null` for the orchestrator seat and `{"error":"roster:seat-scope"}` for a worker. In a courier-only model where the operator boots seats out of band, **a seat cannot tell "the peer isn't booted" from "the peer is booted and hasn't acted"**, and cannot verify its own credential is bound and listening. An empty inbox is indistinguishable from a transport fault, so a shakedown can prove a *successful* hop but never that a *missing* hop is genuinely missing. Worth a scoped roster view (peers addressable by this seat) and a self-identity echo.

**What worked.** Rejected relays persist in the `audit` view while staying out of the default inbox — rejection is durable, not silently dropped, which is what made this diagnosable at all. The conductor stamps `from`/`role` on readback and the seats confirmed they could not assert either.

**Master-side drafting errors this exposed (mine, not frank's).** The boot charters specified `CEREMONY_TIER: tiny` — the trap above, self-inflicted. `STEP-3-LANE4-KICKOFF-NESTED.md` §7 names `PARENT_DISPATCH_ID` as the lineage field in a table and twice in prose; **no such field exists on the wire** (`headers.parent_hint` + `headers.IN_REPLY_TO`). Both seats caught it independently. The w1 charter also gave the worker-return leg `parent_hint` equal to **its own** dispatch id — the degenerate self-parenting case §7's unique-id guard exists to prevent; w1 refused to guess between a charter and its governing plan, which is exactly right. **Lesson: an authority-of-record that names wire fields must be validated against the live tool schema, not against its own prose.**

## Battle report — attribution drift: a synthesis relay invented a ratified requirement's wording and used it to close an owner's open question (2026-07-25, lane-4 esc1 fork)

**The defect.** Master's operator fork packet closed m-10's expressly-open `epoch/lease` scope question by asserting: *"VP r7 required 'exact resumed-context identity incl. the model-visible continuation input and the settlement snapshot' — it enumerates exactly two components."* **VP r7 contains no such sentence.** Its actual text (`step3-arch-packet/RECONCILE-orchestrator-reviewer-20260721-073500.md:62`) reads *"no schema or digest recipe for the claimed exact round/context identity"* plus *"for example a predecessor/round/log-prefix/context digest vector"* — a demand for a recipe and an **illustrative** vector. The VP caught it as VP2-F2 and stated it had **not** selected either branch.

**Why it is a distinct class, not a typo.** The three dispatches that *asked* the owners the question attributed the phrase **correctly** — to the VP's question. The corruption happened in the **synthesis**, where a correctly-attributed review prompt was silently promoted to ratified requirement text and then used as authority to overrule an owner. So the failure is not misreading a source; it is **provenance decaying across a summarization hop** — exactly the class the r12-F1 finding (a fabricated review history in an authority-bearing status line) belongs to, recurring one round later inside a packet about honest provenance.

**The harm is asymmetric and specific.** A false citation in a *narrative* line costs credibility. A false citation used to **close a decision** costs the decision: m-10 had correctly refused to close its own scope question and routed it up; master closed it with invented authority; had the VP not re-read VP r7 verbatim, an unproven epoch/lease fencing property would have been silently excluded from a durability exit gate on the strength of a quote that did not exist.

**Standing rule adopted — the resolvable-citation rule.** *An authority claim of the form "X required Y" is not filed until the author has re-read X's bytes in this turn and can point to the line containing Y.* A citation that names an artifact but paraphrases its demand is a **claim**, not a citation. Corollary: **"including" never licenses an exhaustive reading** — if a decision turns on a set being closed, the closure must be found in the source or obtained from the owner, never inferred from an enumeration's shape.

**What frank mechanizes here.** This is the strongest case yet for a courier-enforced citation primitive: a relay that asserts a quotation carries `{artifact_digest, locator, quoted_bytes}` and the conductor **verifies the quoted bytes resolve at that locator in that digest** before `accepted`. Every input needed is already in the trusted store — the frozen artifact digests are lock constituents. Under that gate this defect is **unfileable**, and it is a deterministic check on honest labeling, not a judgment call: precisely the confusion-not-malice threat model. Companion to the computability check above (that one binds *expectations* to a derivation; this one binds *authority claims* to bytes).

**And the second-order finding — I re-created a defect I had just been corrected on.** After the VP's F1 required m-9 to re-file its answer under a fresh unique id, master dispatched m-10 and m-3 **without supplying distinct answer ids**, so both filed self-parented answers reusing their request's `DISPATCH_ID` — two fresh instances of the same defect, one round after learning it. The lesson is that a correction applied to **the instance** and not to **the dispatch template** does not hold: a lineage rule that depends on every recipient independently inventing a unique id will fail at the rate recipients forget. **Frank fixes this by construction** — the courier assigns the reply's identity, so a seat cannot reuse its request's id even by trying.

## Battle report — retrieval-before-reasoning: five times in one session the answer was already written down and an agent reasoned instead of reading (2026-07-25, lane-4 esc1)

**The operator's finding, in their words:** *"stuff like this shouldn't happen — need to prompt agents to first look for answers, either in documents already written, or online for external sources, or other resources provided."*

**The five instances, all from one escalation.** Not one was a hallucination in the usual sense; every answer existed in reach.
1. **A ratified requirement's wording invented.** Master attributed to VP r7 a two-member enumeration it does not contain. The real text was in a document master had *already cited correctly* in three earlier dispatches — the corruption happened in the synthesis hop. (Recorded separately as the attribution-drift report.)
2. **A citation pointed at the wrong half of a contract.** Master cited m-8 `:223` (response-side SSE corpora) as the *request*-capture surface. The correct fixture (14, the on-wire capture) is in the same file, 100 lines earlier. m-8 caught it.
3. **A ratified operator decision described as a new constraint.** Master told the operator that `context_digest` would "lock in" a replay-not-regenerate constraint on compaction. The operator had **already ratified the opposite stance** in the m-9 grill (stage-4 §7.1:88 — eliminate the reload boundary so a stale summary can *never* be replayed), and a later lock constituent (r17 §3:336) had flipped it to make replay the correct path. Two documents, opposite postures — surfaced only because the operator said *"I believe I've answered this before."* **Correction, filed minutes later:** master then escalated this as "no recorded supersession exists," which was FALSE — see instance 6, which is the sharpest failure in this list.
4. **A four-round multi-owner negotiation that a single retrieval would have shortcut.** The mechanism that may collapse the expensive half of this escalation — *the compaction summary is itself a logged record, so rebuilding is replaying, not re-deciding* — sat in m-9's own answer as two separate stated facts. Neither m-9, m-10, m-3 nor master drew the conclusion. It took the operator opening a live session's JSONL and observing that compaction changes what is *sent*, never what is *recorded*.
5. **General prior art recited while domain-specific vetted prior art sat in the repo.** Asked how other harnesses handle this, master produced Git/Raft/SQLite/Postgres/Kafka from general knowledge. m-9 had already run an **8-harness production survey + an arxiv/industry sweep + two operator-provided field audits** (`2026-07-19-mvp-full-worker.md` §9, GRILL-ratified), naming rejected practices with pinned commits — deepagents *fabricating* a tool result for a dangling call, opaque compaction capsules, vector-store transcript retrieval, training-gated compaction. Strictly better material, already paid for, not consulted until the operator asked twice.

6. **An absence reported from a search that had already found the thing — the worst of the six, because it is purely mechanical.** Master escalated to m-9 that the stage-4/r17 conflict had "no recorded supersession," having run `grep -niE "supersede|...|§7\.1|..." delta.md | head -12`. That pattern matched **39 lines, including line 373 — `## §5 — §7.1 invariant supersession`, the exact answer.** The first twelve matches were all revision-history noise, so `head -12` discarded the answer and master reported the truncation as the corpus. m-9's reply had to hand back a section master's own search had located. **This is the second absence-from-incomplete-search error in the same session** — the first was leaning "strike `context_digest` as vestigial" on a zero-occurrence lexical search, refuted by three owners.

**Why it is a class and not six slips.** Reasoning is fast, always available, and *feels* complete. Retrieval is slow, needs the right query, and frequently returns nothing — so the expected-value calculation at the moment of answering favours reasoning, every time, for every agent. **This is an economics problem, not a discipline problem**, which is why "be more careful" will not fix it and a mechanism will.

**Standing rule adopted — retrieval-before-reasoning, with a sharp trigger.** Three claim classes may not be answered from reasoning; each requires a retrieval first:
- **"Document X says Y"** ⇒ read X's bytes this turn. (Already covered by the resolvable-citation rule; restated here as one member of a family.)
- **"We decided Y"** ⇒ locate the decision — grill lock, ratification relay, amendment — and cite it. A remembered decision is a hypothesis.
- **"The field does Y"** ⇒ check whether *we already surveyed it* before reaching for general knowledge; then external sources.
Plus a fourth that instance 4 earns: **"can this happen / does this occur"** ⇒ inspect a real instance if one is reachable. An operator reading one live session log outperformed five agents reasoning about the same mechanism.
Design opinions and derivations are exempt — the trigger is claims about *what is recorded*, not about what is true.

**And the hard mechanical rule instance 6 earns — no absence claim from a truncated or narrow search.** A search whose *absence* you intend to report may not be piped through `head`, `tail`, or any cap: read the match count first, and if the set is large, widen the reading rather than the filter. More generally: **a search that returns nothing licenses "I did not find it," never "it does not exist"** — the corpus is not the query's output. Both absence errors this session would have been caught by that single rule, and neither required judgment to avoid.

**A concrete indexing gap this exposes, fixable today.** `PRIOR-ART.md` is the designated home for all prior-art content, but the 8-harness compaction survey lives in an m-9 design doc and nothing points to it from the question it answers. There is **no index from question → prior answer**, so retrieval only succeeds for an agent that already knows the artifact exists — i.e. exactly the agent who does not need it. Every survey, grill lock and ratified decision needs to be reachable by the question it settles, not only by the document that houses it.

**What frank mechanizes — the operator's "frank will fix this", made concrete.** Three layers, cheapest first:
1. **Reject the un-retrieved claim.** Extending the citation primitive from the attribution-drift report: a relay asserting a member of the three claim classes must carry `{artifact_digest, locator, quoted_bytes}`, and the conductor verifies the bytes resolve at that locator in that digest before `accepted`. The frozen artifact digests are already lock constituents, so every input exists. Under that gate, instances 1, 2, 3 and 5 are **unfileable** — and instance 6 becomes unfileable too, since "no record of X exists" is itself a claim about the corpus that the courier can check directly against the store.
2. **Make retrieval cheaper than reasoning.** Seats already reach the store via `project`/`read`. With the governed corpus in the store under digests, "find the prior decision about compaction on resume" becomes a courier query rather than a grep across a directory tree. **Lower the retrieval cost and the behaviour changes without requiring discipline** — this is the layer that actually fixes the economics, and it is the one worth building.
3. **Index by question, not by document.** A `settles:` back-index over grill locks, surveys and ratifications, so a claim class can be resolved by asking what governs it. This is what instance 4 needed and what no amount of care would have supplied.

**Companion to the two rules already standing:** the *computability check* binds a frozen expectation to a named derivation; the *resolvable-citation rule* binds an authority claim to bytes; this one binds an **answer** to a retrieval. All three are deterministic checks on honest labeling rather than judgment calls — the confusion-not-malice threat model applied to the agent's own epistemics.

## Standing rule — a verification line must be MEASURED after the action, never derived from the intent of the edit (2026-07-26, lane-4 esc1)

**Scope, stated narrowly at the VP's instruction (LANE4-ESC1-VP5).** The evidence for *this* rule is one incident: master's ratification record asserted that a `ROADMAP.md` paragraph had been "rewrapped to the file's 108-char convention" when the edited line measured **181** characters. The cause was mechanical — the insertion did not begin on a fresh line, so the preceding paragraph's tail joined the new text and the rewrap started *after* the join. The claim described what the edit was *meant* to do; nobody measured the file. Caught by VP review, not by the author.

**Deliberately NOT absorbed here:** the fabricated-relay-timestamp incident and the `head`-truncated absence claim each already carry their own recorded mechanism (drift enforcement in relay-lint v2.8.8.3; the no-absence-from-a-capped-search rule). They are *adjacent* — all three are claims the author could have checked and didn't — but conflating them into one rule would overstate this entry's evidence and duplicate two that already exist.

**The rule.** Any verification or action-record line describing a disk action must be produced by **measuring the artifact after the write** — length, hash, count, presence — never by restating the intent of the edit. An unmeasured "I did X" in a verification section is a claim, not evidence, and it is the weakest kind because the author is the only party positioned to check it cheaply.

**Why it belongs with the citation primitive rather than beside it.** This is machine-checkable in the same way and from the same inputs: lengths, hashes and counts are all computable by the courier from the artifact the relay names. A relay asserting `sha256(X)=…`, `max_line(X)=…` or `count(X)=…` can be **verified before `accepted`**, exactly as a quotation claim can be resolved against a digest. Companion to the *resolvable-citation* rule (authority claims → bytes) and the *computability check* (frozen expectations → a named derivation): that pair binds claims about **other people's artifacts**; this one binds claims about **the author's own writes**, which is the gap the first two leave open.
