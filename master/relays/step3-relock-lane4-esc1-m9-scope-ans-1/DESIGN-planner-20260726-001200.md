## Q2 — the honest floor, and what breaks below it
**Floor = a typed, append-only run journal per run** (the reviewer's enumerated field list) **+ a per-record checksum** (torn/corrupt detection) **+ a last-good checkpoint at each settled tool-round boundary** (where valid history ends) **+ the per-run writer fence.** This is exactly what a real harness on SQLite/Postgres gets from its storage engine (per-record checksum + WAL/last-good marker) — "like any other harness," honestly.
- **Below the FULL apparatus but at this floor: nothing the MVP threat model admits breaks.** Chaining catches only malice (excluded); rotation/cross-segment/seal are correct-at-scale and unexecuted at one turn. The torn-write problem — the thing that actually motivates durability — is solved by per-record checksum + the round checkpoint.
- **Below the floor (raw JSONL, no checksum, no marker): the torn-write problem returns** — a plain append-only file cannot distinguish "ends here because the turn ended" from "ends here because the machine died mid-write." So "like any other harness" is only safe if the harness's *storage engine* supplies the checksum + last-good marker; a bare JSONL with neither does not. **I do not recommend going below the floor.**
- **"Tamper-evidence" is NOT my justification for anything I keep** — per your instruction. Every FLOOR element ties to torn-write or the concurrent-writer confusion race, both admitted; nothing I keep rests on malice.
