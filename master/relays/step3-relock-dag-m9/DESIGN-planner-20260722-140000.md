## What changed

**§1.4 gains STEP 0 — a terminal-STATE guard, evaluated on the RAW BYTES before any parse.** Whenever the last accepted record's `kind` is `segment_seal`, the next physical byte sequence `B` in the file is disposed of here and nowhere else:
- `B` **byte-identical to the accepted seal's own raw line and immediately adjacent** ⇒ **collapse** (the single benign re-append exception, same effect as step 2), then re-apply step 0;
- **any other byte sequence whatsoever** ⇒ **whole-run fail-close** — explicitly and by enumeration: a **torn/unparseable fragment**, a **conflicting same-`seq`** line, a **gapped or regressed `seq`**, a **wrong `prev_digest`**, a **second `segment_seal`**, and a perfectly-formed continuing record. **None of these may reach steps 1, 3, 4 or 5**;
- **EOF** ⇒ the segment ends normally at its seal.

The remaining branches renumber 1–6 (self-consistency · adjacent-collapse · conflicting duplicate · gap/regression · chain · accept) with **no semantic change**. The rationale paragraph now states the ordering explicitly — a rule's covered domain in a first-match-wins algorithm is a property of where it sits, and the four shadowed shapes are named as what r4 actually did (return *a clean prefix ending at the seal*, the opposite of what row 11a promised).

**Why my fixture didn't catch it, stated plainly:** the r4 battery exercised only the perfectly-formed stale round — precisely the one shape that survives steps 1–5 and therefore the only one that reached branch 6. A single positive case over a universal claim proved nothing about the other four.

**§10's terminal-seal battery is rebuilt as SIX post-seal shapes with ONE disposition**, and each case must assert **which branch fired**, not merely that recovery failed — so a regression to the r4 ordering fails the battery instead of passing it by coincidence. Cases 2–5 are exactly the four you named, each annotated with the branch that used to shadow it. Positive control: the byte-identical adjacent seal repeat still collapses and the segment stays valid. In every case the fixture also asserts **the boundary digest still agrees**, so the design cannot quietly start leaning on the digest for a job step 0 is doing.

**One residual named rather than masked:** a **torn** re-append of the seal is not byte-identical, so it fails closed rather than collapsing. That is deliberate. A torn post-seal fragment is indistinguishable from a stale writer's torn append, and under a rule whose entire purpose is to make a second writer loud, the honest disposition for an indistinguishable input is the fail-closed one. The cost is a rare false fault on a benign torn retry of the seal itself; the alternative is a silent pass on a real double-write. If you read that trade the other way, say so — it is a genuine choice, not an oversight.
