**Why `0/0` is right on the merits, not just on the sum.** The budget's unit is *governed turns* and *tool calls*. A refused admission never opens a turn and never dispatches a call — it is an admission-gate event, exactly as m-3 characterises it. Assigning it any positive weight would be the actual defect: it would count toward a budget of things that did not occur, and it would silently consume headroom from records that do perform work. `0/0` is the only honest weight, and it happens to be the one that leaves the ratified invariant untouched.

**One separation that must stay explicit, or `0/0` becomes a hiding place.** `sample_weight` is a *budget allocation*, not an *execution-cost measure*. A `0/0` record still executes, still takes wall clock, and still contributes to the §7 overhead rule (per-metric p95 ceilings plus the p50 added-wall-clock band). In my own suite the two are already separate — the overhead denominator is `baseline-workload.json`'s `repetitions`, never `sample_weight` — so nothing in my structure conflates them. I name it because a future consumer that derived the overhead denominator from `sample_weight` would silently exclude both refused records from the measurement, and a weight-0 record is precisely the shape that makes that error invisible. Keeping the denominator sourced from the baseline artifact closes it.

## (a) The 12-record coupling — CONCUR

**The reuse costs `xit-dur-1` zero additional weight, and that is the strongest thing in its favour.** The positive arm reads m-10's admission/assign-gate observation at the correct epoch/lease. `xit-dur-1` *already* performs exactly that admission: it crashes at cut 4, recovers, and resumes through a successor turn admitted at the current epoch under a valid lease. The observation is not an errand the fixture is being sent on — it is a property of the path it already walks. Its pinned turns (`dur1-t1`, `dur1-t2`) and its 3/12 allocation absorb it with no additional turn and no additional call. That is why this reuse is not the cheap-shortcut kind.

**Expressible in my structure, concretely.** The positive arm needs no eleventh record because it is an additional expected observation on an existing one: `xit-dur-1`'s `capture_requirements` gains the admission/assign-gate capture, and its `expected_canonical_rows` gains the corresponding rows. Both are existing slots doing their ordinary job. The two negatives are ordinary records with their own `input_artifact_sha256`, `fault_injection_point` and `evidence_locator`. Nothing here asks my schema to carry something it cannot.

**The honest cost, named rather than buried: discrimination.** `xit-dur-1` now serves two legs, so a failure there fails both and the signal does not by itself say which property broke. This is worth stating because **m-10's own precedent runs the other way on fixture reuse** — `FX-M10-CAP` is required NOT to reuse the `resume_frame_overflow` fixture, on the reasoning that the cap terminal's lifecycle is "proven on its own bytes" and "neither's fixture proves the other's." I do not think that precedent controls here, and I say why rather than leaving the tension unremarked: that ruling separates two *distinct terminals* each needing its own lifecycle proof, whereas this observation is intrinsic to the resume path under test. The distinction is real, but it is fine enough that it should be on the record rather than discovered later.

The mitigation is already in m-3's ruling and I rely on it: the precondition is **binary and testable**, and failure is a **hard stop with a separately-reviewed 13-record successor** — never a silent cardinality change. That is the right failure mode, and it is what makes the coupling acceptable rather than merely convenient.
