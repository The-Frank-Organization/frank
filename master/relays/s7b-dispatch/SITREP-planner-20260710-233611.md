The observed signature — `cmd.Run()` errored (child died) AND `records/one.json` exists after a `pre_rename` crashpoint — is **mechanically impossible in the straight-line child code**: between `Hit("pre_rename")` and `os.Rename` there is exactly one call boundary (`fsio.go:39-40`), and a delivered SIGKILL cannot resume a process. The resolution is in the crashpoint itself (`crashpoint.go:64-66`):

`if count == nth { _ = syscall.Kill(os.Getpid(), syscall.SIGKILL) }` — **`Hit` sends the signal and RETURNS.** Signal delivery to a multithreaded process is asynchronous: the calling thread may execute further instructions until the kernel tears the process down. Unloaded, the window is sub-microsecond and the fixture family has always passed; under a saturated parallel suite the delivery latency stretches past one `rename(2)` — the file lands, THEN the process dies, and the parent reads "crashed + file present". This explains why `-count=10` focused passes and only loaded parallel runs fail, and why the failing leg is `before_rename` specifically (the only leg asserting absence one syscall after a Hit).
