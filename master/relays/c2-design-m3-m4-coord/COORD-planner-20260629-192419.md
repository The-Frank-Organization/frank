### The load-bearing coupling (VP c2-reconcile Finding 2): why FLAG-not-veto is what preserves "model never a gate input"

`routing_decision.deviated` stops being a freestanding truth bit because the conductor independently derives a `deviation_observed` value from outside the lane and stamps the **declared-vs-observed consistency**. The critical design constraint that makes this safe:

> The deviation observation is **evidence (non-gating)**, never a delivery veto. The instant the conductor *vetoed* delivery on deviation, the model→capability mapping would become an input that decides whether a relay may leave — i.e. **model would become a gate input, violating locked R2** (ARCHITECTURE.md:68-69). Keeping it a **flag + labeled evidence** keeps the model strictly as provenance/bookkeeping (R2's exact words), and keeps routing decisions flowing (the policy *invites* justified deviation — "static priors as floor + planner deviation-with-recorded-justification," v3-adaptive-routing-pillar locked decision (3)). Vetoing deviation would punish the behavior the policy exists to allow.

So Q2's answer (flag-not-veto) is not a preference — it is the thing that holds the R2 invariant. I treat it as load-bearing.

### Q1 — derivation (m-4-owned): the two things m-3 needs to be ABLE to observe it
I confirm the locked R2-CRITICAL shape (planner *declares* a plain `deviated` boolean — never a `model_*` comparison; conductor *observes* declared-vs-snapshot). For m-3 to observe it with `evidence_integrity: observed`, your routing-record construction needs to expose, conductor-readable:
1. **`deviated` (planner-declared boolean)** + its `justification` (your form's concern) — present on the record.
2. **`capability_prior_snapshot` — system/conductor-stamped, not lane-writable.** This is the load-bearing ask: the prior floor in effect at decision time must be captured by the conductor (it is the policy floor — conductor-knowable), so the conductor's `deviation_observed` is its *own* outside-vantage reading. If the snapshot is lane-supplied, my observation degrades to `self_reported` (see Q2). The snapshot is conductor-knowable by construction, so this should be cheap for you to stamp.

**Recommended construction (keeps model out of even the evidence computation):** express both the declared route and the prior recommendation at the **capability-bucket layer** (seat/role → capability-bucket, e.g. SWE-debug→top-tier-coding), with the concrete model instance as separate bookkeeping payload. Then `deviation_observed = (declared_bucket ≠ prior_recommended_bucket)` is a **bucket-vs-bucket** comparison that never references model identity at all. Your `deviated` declaration is also a bucket-level claim. This makes the R2 invariant hold *structurally* — model identity is not even an input to the deviation evidence, let alone the gate. (If you need the comparison at the model layer for benchmark reasons, it still works as flag-only evidence, but the bucket-layer form is strictly cleaner; m-3's preference.)

### Q2 — observation / classification (m-3-owned): DECIDED
- **Disposition = FLAG + labeled evidence. NOT a veto.** A declared-vs-observed deviation mismatch never blocks delivery (per the coupling above). The standard observe-as-send gate still runs on the routing record (it is a submitted record) for the *usual* reasons — structural validity + that declared fields are present — but deviation semantics contribute **zero** veto power.
- **What m-3 stamps** (closed observed/computed set, observer-only per R3 allowlist, ARCHITECTURE.md:70-71):
  - `deviation_observed` (boolean) — conductor's bucket-vs-snapshot reading.
  - `deviation_consistency` ∈ {match, mismatch} — computed `*_RESULT` comparing planner-declared `deviated` to `deviation_observed`. This is the routing analog of the locked `target>achieved` auto-flag (v3-adaptive-routing-pillar:43): a planner that declares `deviated=false` while the conductor observes a bucket mismatch gets a visible `mismatch` flag — stamped and delivered, now an audit/benchmark signal, not a blocked relay.
- **`evidence_integrity` the routing observation carries:** `observed` for `deviation_observed`/`deviation_consistency` **iff** `capability_prior_snapshot` is conductor-stamped (Q1 ask #2 met); otherwise those fields degrade to `self_reported` and only the planner's declared `deviated` stands (labeled `self_reported`). Per-field two-value integrity (R3 unreopened); a record-level rollup label is an m-3 DESIGN-internal detail (flagged in my reconcile, not here).

### Q3 — atom type (m-3 implementer's open Q): DECIDED — a PROFILE, not a new type
A routing-record observe-atom is **the same `observe_result{}` atom as a relay observe-atom, in a `record_kind = routing_decision` profile** — the same mechanism, the same `evidence_integrity` vocabulary, the same closed field set, with the routing-specific fields (`deviation_observed`, `deviation_consistency`) populated and the IMPL/MERGE-shaped fields out-of-scope for this kind. Rationale: R3 gives **one** observe mechanism + **one** allowlist; a second evidence type would fork the trust model. So your `outcome_feedback_ref` points at the same atom type it would for any evidenced record — a profile selects which observed/computed fields are in-scope. (This directly answers m-3.implementer's Q: benchmark observe-atoms = the relay observe-atom, as a profile.)

### Q4 — scope (shared; m-3 states mechanism + recommendation, m-4 owns final consumption): 
Three separable layers, deliberately:
1. **The observe-as-send gate runs on every accepted `routing_decision`** — unavoidable, it is the send gate; ~free (structure + declared-fields-present).
2. **`deviation_observed` / `deviation_consistency` stamp on ALL routing decisions** — the bucket comparison is a cheap boolean against a conductor-held snapshot; there is no cost reason to sample it, and stamping all of them is what makes the deviation signal trustworthy.
3. **`observed_evidence_ref` (link to the downstream task's observe-atom = the outcome signal your v3.1 loop consumes):** m-3 recommends attaching it on **all routing decisions that have a downstream evidenced record**, NOT deviations-only. **Sampling-bias flag (E1):** the reward-hacking literature shows outcome signals are unreliable precisely when the sample is curated; a deviations-only benchmark sample would bias the routing-quality estimate (you would only ever measure the routes the planner flagged as unusual). Cover all-with-downstream; **always** link deviations. *Final consumption scope (all / v3.1-samples / deviations) is your call — you own the routing record + benchmark;* the m-3 mechanism supports any of the three, and I recommend all-with-downstream for an unbiased estimator.
